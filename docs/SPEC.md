# spec: the credits engine as built

the current reading of the owner's intent and of the system that implements it. docs/DECISIONS.md is the dated decision log and the authority on intent; this file cites its numbers. docs/STATUS.md holds the plan, the freeze table and the state of the branch. precedence between sources: AGENTS.md. the vocabulary is "balance", never "exit".

## 1. the product

CC is a coin whose trading fees can fund a public bid for Credits while the Core is active, unpaused and sufficiently funded. anyone may sell eligible Credits to the engine at its quoted bid, and the engine may sweep eligible listings on approved venues; the bid gap can pay the caller. the engine composes 80 Credits into a Statement, holds it, and sells it at a price that starts above cost and falls to a floor the owner sets. after artist and operator cuts, the sale setting earmarks a share for a later CC buyback and puts the rest in the buying pot; a sale does not itself burn CC. when the balance token is live and its module can pay, eligible Statements redeem into it by rating and the engine may buy Credits with it by score at a flat posted price, par minus `balanceDiscountBps` (1,000 at launch, decision 164). redemption can move a share of the fee reserve to a later buyback; it does not itself guarantee a burn. the owner changes economic settings with no delay; holders trust the owner key, while one way locks and successor migration can narrow or move that power. the engine launches before the balance token; the balance bid and chosen composition ship built and switched off; the credit sale window and redeem switch are on at launch, and redemption needs a usable module. the balance token is held by its module, not the Core. (decisions 99 to 117, 155, 163, 164, 176, 178 and 186.)

## 4. the bid

the rate is wei per 100 points (`RATE_SCORE` = 1,000,000 score units). `ethRate()` is the read: the price state lowered to the pot, where the pot is `ethPot` plus the pot share of unbooked fee income. a credit of score `s` is paid `ethRate() * s / RATE_SCORE` (`bidForScore(s)`); `ethPerPoint()` is `ethRate() / 100`; `bidOf(id)` is `bidForScore(scoreOf(id))`. the eth doors read the score from the CreditScore contract (`scoreOf(seedOf, timestampOf)`, the same source the balance side reads), 800,000 to 8,000,000, never 0. the doors book first, then read the bid of the booked pot.

| rule | as built |
|---|---|
| opening | provisional `rateStart` is 4.65e15 wei per 100 points, pending the deployment-day market refresh; in [1, uint64.max] and at most `rateCap` |
| per credit | a credit of score `s` is paid `ethRate() * s / RATE_SCORE` (`bidForScore(s)`, `bidOf(id)`); `buyListing` caps the quote at it; `adopt` records `ethPrice() * s / RATE_SCORE`, at least 1 wei |
| price state | while the booked buying room funds one 100-point bid at `lastFillRate` plus its artist payment, the stored rate climbs per minute since its checkpoint: `recoveryClimbPerMinBps` below the recovery level, `climbPerMinBps` at or above it, compounded, continuous at the crossing, up to `min(ceiling, rateCap, booked buying room)`. below that threshold, no elapsed climb or ceiling loosening accrues. a refill checkpoints the old state before adding ETH and begins a fresh funded interval. the climb is not scaled by the pot multiplier |
| pot multiplier | `m = clamp(pages, 1, climbMultiplierCapBps / 10,000)`, the pages the booked pot covers at the last fill rate, where one page is `pointsPerPage` points costing `lastFillRate * pointsPerPage / 100`: `pages = ethPot / (lastFillRate * pointsPerPage / 100)`. the last fill rate is the rate credits last cleared at; it changes only on a fill or `setRate`. `m` scales the ceiling's idle loosening only; the per minute climb is unscaled. `climbMultiplierCapBps` is the owner cap, 10,000 to 65,535, launch 40,000 (4x). `climbMultiplier()` returns `m` for the eth instance. `pointsPerPage` 35,200 is 80 times the historical 440-point collection mean, separate from the fixed 100-point bid unit and funding test |
| peak | the highest recent fill rate, halved every `peakHalfLife` since the last fill or `setRate`; every fill sets it to `max(fill rate, peak now)` |
| ceiling | `ceilBps` of `max(last fill rate, peak now)` grown by `idleLoosenBps * m` per full `idleStepSeconds` interval since the later of the last fill and the funded clock restart; an underfunded interval adds no steps |
| clamp | the read uses `ethPot` plus the pot share of unbooked fee income the next `_book` adds, less `reserve()`, divided by `1 + buyToArtistBps / 10,000`, so a displayed bid can pay the artist royalty on top. the climb target and funding test use booked `ethPot` alone, with the same reserve and royalty adjustment; pending fees have no known arrival time and earn no idle climb. at the approved 0.0097 ETH opening rate, the 100-point funding threshold is 0.0099425 ETH above the 0.1 ETH reserve, so booked pot 0.1099425 ETH is the boundary. this is a funding proxy, not a live marketplace listing check. a sale whose running total passes the pot reverts whole |
| drop | a credit of score `s` bought at price state `p` sets it to `p * (10,000 - dropPer100PointsBps * s / RATE_SCORE) / 10,000` (the fall bounded at 10,000), at least `dropFloorBps` of the price state at the bucket's first fill. the drop tracks the eth spent |
| floor rate | `max(price state, peak now)`: the rate under every price floor of the engine (`floorRate()`) |
| reset | `setRate(rate)` restates the stored rate, the last fill rate and the peak, and starts a fresh funded ceiling clock; the actual last-fill timestamp remains unchanged |

the same price state machine runs as two instances, sharing every climb and drop setting above (`climbPerMinBps`, `recoveryClimbPerMinBps`, `recoveryGapBps`, `dropPer100PointsBps`, `dropFloorBps`, `ceilBps`, `idleLoosenBps`, `peakHalfLife`) and the pot multiplier `m`, each instance reading its own clamp as the multiplier's pot. the eth bid is the instance above: the unit is wei, the cap is `rateCap`, the clamp is the pot, the opening value is `rateStart`, and `setRate` resets it. the second instance is the shared instance of value mode (decision 164): its rate is bps of par, the cap is `balanceBidCapBps`, 10,000 at launch and adjustable up to uint32.max, the floor and opening value is `10,000 - balanceBidMarginBps`, and in value mode it is the eth bid's price state, read by `sharedRate()` and multiplied by the controller's value per point. the balance bid does not read it: `balanceRate()` is the posted `10,000 - balanceDiscountBps` and `balanceBidOf(id)` is `score * unitPerPoint * balanceRate() / 10,000` (section 9). a change of `balanceBidMarginBps` that raises the floor above the stored rate resets the instance to the new floor.

value mode (`valueModeOn`, decision 148, off at launch). the eth bid prices off the shared balance instance: `ethRate() = min(bps * valuePerPoint(false) * 100 / 10,000, rateCap)`, lowered to the room (`ethPot` plus the pending pot share less the reserve), where `bps` is the shared instance climbed to now with its clamp at `balanceBidCapBps` rather than the module's funds, and `valuePerPoint(false)` is the controller's wei value of one point at the protected buy price, read by staticcall with 200,000 gas. a failed, short, zero or not ok answer yields a zero ETH quote: `ethRate`, `ethPrice` and `bidOf` read zero, and an ETH acquisition cannot proceed at a stale value rate (provisional repair under decision 196, superseding decision 159 C02 for these doors); the controller's sale doors still revert `NoQuote`. `sellForBalance`, compose, redemption, the buyback and `book` never read the eth bid. a fill through `sellForEth` or `buyListing` drops the shared instance by the per credit drop, floored at `10,000 - balanceBidMarginBps`, and leaves the eth instance as it was (`ValueRateFill(spent, rateBps, pot)` in place of `EthRateFill`). the instance climbs in value mode whatever `balanceBidOn` and the module; turning `valueModeOn` on resets it to the floor, a settings edit or a module set leaves it. `adopt` uses the last good value-mode ETH floor for its cost basis during a quote outage and refuses adoption when no such basis exists; `sellForBalance` records the value-mode rate as the cost basis. neither accounting rule opens the ETH bid. the balance token payout is lowered to the eth bid for the same score valued in tokens at the protected sale price per token, `min(posted, bidForScore(score) * 1e18 / P_sell)` (decision 165): paying tokens is the engine selling tokens, so the payout is worth at most the eth bid when sold and a cross-currency round trip cannot extract a spot that leads the reference. the conversion and the `min` are the controller view `balanceClampFor(score, posted)`, read by a bounded staticcall; the Core returns zero payout when that bounded read fails or the reference is unusable, so the BALANCE purchase does not complete. `balanceBidOf` and `balanceBidForScore` report the clamped amount, so the view equals the fill. outside value mode the payout is par, unchanged.

the controller's protected price: `referencePrice` starts at the pool spot when `setBalancePoolKey` runs, provided the live spot is within 4.5 percent of the owner's expected spot independently of `tolBps` (decision 161), and `refreshReference()` (public, also run first by `buy`, `buyCredit` and `buyCredits`) moves it toward the spot by at most `min(referenceRateBpsPerHour * elapsed / 3600, referenceStepBps)` bps of the reference. buys price off `P_buy = min(sampledSpot, referencePrice * (10,000 + tolBps) / 10,000)`, sales off `P_sell = max(sampledSpot, referencePrice * (10,000 - tolBps) / 10,000)`; the executable spot is sampled at a prior block. the quote is usable while `referenceMaxStale` is nonzero, the reference is at most `referenceMaxStale` old, both recent pool liquidity samples are at least `minLiquidity`, and the spot and the module unit read. `spotPrice()` reports the spot. the eth bid pays at most `cap * score * unit * P_buy` and the sale floor asks at least `(10,000 + spreadBps) * cap * score * unit * P_sell` over the same base, with `P_buy <= P_sell`, so an eth round trip at one executable quote cannot profit. a balance round trip takes the payout in tokens, whose par value realizes at the live spot; the balance payout is clamped to the eth bid at `P_sell` (decision 165) so it cannot profit at one executable quote. when a pool move persists across later reference refreshes, a Credit bought at an older ask can be sold into a higher later bid after the return lock. the configured spread does not bound that gain. the owner requires a pricing change before phase-two activation (decision 197); the replay and real-swap limitations are recorded in docs/reviews/LAUNCH-OWNER-REVIEW.md.


### ETH purchase budget

Both `sellForEth` overloads and every adapter listing share one ledger. At the first successful purchase in `floor(timestamp / bucketSeconds)`, snapshot the booked buying pot and set `spendBudget = floor(snapshotPot * spendCapBps / 10,000)`. Fee booking happens first; buyback and redeem reserves are excluded. Count the complete ETH outflow, including listing keeper payment. The first purchase of a bucket always passes whatever its cost; the budget is still snapshotted and the spend recorded. Each later credit raises `SpendCapReached` when spent plus cost exceeds the budget, and the entire batch or failed venue purchase rolls back. Equality succeeds. A budget below one credit's price, or rounded to zero, therefore permits one purchase per bucket. Balance-token purchases are excluded.

Same-bucket deposits and fee income cannot enlarge this snapshot. `setRate` and unrelated settings preserve it and the used amount. Changing the cap immediately reprices the original snapshot, preserving spent. At 10,000 bps enforcement is bypassed, including against later deposits, but outflow still counts if enforcement is restored. A duration edit first expires the ledger under its old duration; an active ledger is translated to the new bucket with its snapshot and used amount retained. Both bid instances' active drop-floor anchors translate likewise; expired anchors clear. An ordinary new bucket resets lazily on its first successful purchase.

`spendState()` returns `(bucket, budget, spent)` for the current bucket: the stored values while the bucket is open, otherwise zero spent and the budget `(ethPot + pending) * spendCapBps / 10,000`, where `pending` is the pot share of the unbooked fee income the next `_book` adds (decision 147). A purchase books that income first, so this is the pot the first purchase of the bucket snapshots and the budget it enforces.

`ethRate()` and `bidOf()` remain price quotes. A quoted purchase after the first of its bucket can revert because the remaining aggregate budget is insufficient. A bucket cap is not a rolling-window cap: adjacent buckets can both spend around their boundary. The counters and stored budget are checked uint128 values; theoretical outflow or budgets above uint128.max revert instead of truncating.

The exposure review `docs/reviews/EXPOSURE-146-nofloor.md` (2026-10-16, decision 146) runs the launch bid rule with no minute floor (`dropFloorBps` 1) and the spend cap off (`spendCapBps` 10,000) against a 500 credit dump: the compounding drop bounds the dump to 0.86 ETH on a 10 or a 100 ETH pot, the worst minute is 0.44 ETH, no seller is refused while the pot holds ETH, the launch wave clears in one block, recovery is 105 minutes, and steady throughput is 23 credits an hour. Lower `spendCapBps` during an incident.

the pile: one insertion ordered doubly linked list of credit ids, oldest first, with cost basis and acquisition time per credit. every credit enters the pile through `sellForEth` (cost the per-credit bid paid plus its artist royalty), `buyListing` (cost the quote plus its royalty plus the keeper payment), `sellForBalance` (cost `floorRate * score / RATE_SCORE`, the balance royalty is paid in balance token and not recorded) or `adopt` (cost `ethPrice * score / RATE_SCORE`). the royalty is `buyToArtistBps` of the amount paid, on top, decision 163.

## 5. fee intake

the Core is the pool's bounty recipient. `receive()` accepts eth from the hook and the fee escrow only and does nothing else; a plain send from anyone else reverts `UseFund`. every door starts with `_book()`: the eth above `ethPot + ethToBuyback + ethRedeemBurn`, with the door's own `msg.value` excluded, is fee income. the rate is checkpointed first; `payeePpm` of the income is pushed to the payee once `payeeOn`; `feeToRedeemBurnBps` of the rest goes to the redeem burn pot; the remainder goes to the pot. `payeeOn` latches at the first book with income at or after the launch time recorded by the factory plus the current `settings.sniperSeconds` (4,140 at launch), and that book shares nothing, so the anti sniper window's income is never shared (decision 26). the owner or a keeper calls `book()` when the window ends so the latch does not wait for the next door. The latch is one way. This Core setting does not change the pool MEV schedule fixed at launch by the separate config `sniperSeconds`.

other eth enters through doors that book it to the pot whole: `fund()` (payable, anyone, also the predecessor engine on `migrate`) and `collectEscrow()` (claims the Core's escrow credit and books the claim whole to the pot, the refund of a partial buyback fill). `collectEscrow` books the claim whole to the pot. the Core's `receive` is two immutable compares and accepts the hook's 2,300 gas push, so the fee escrow holds buyback refunds in practice; a fee push that ever failed would be booked to the pot without the payee and redeem burn pot shares, an accepted property (decision 125). the payee share is pushed by the book with `SafeTransferLib.forceSafeTransferETH(payee, amount, 50,000)`, so a payee that reverts or runs out of gas never blocks the book. the balance token is held by the module, not the Core, so there is no balance booking door. a stray external `escrow.claim(core, 0)` lands as fee income at the next book.

per 100 eth of volume at launch values: 1.0 eth to the protocol, 9.0 eth to the Core, of which 0 to the payee (payeePpm 0 at launch), 0.9 eth to the reserve, 8.1 eth to the pot.

| `adopt(ids)` | puts credits the Core holds without a record into the pile at the current ETH floor times score, at least 1 wei. In value mode a quote outage uses the last good ETH floor for this accounting basis; adoption reverts if no basis has been established |
| `swapCredit(inId, outId)` | not paused, not retired; `outId` in the pile (`NotInPile`), `inId` owned by the caller and not in a pile (`InPile`), `score(inId) >= score(outId)` (`ScoreTooLow`). the caller burns `swapFeeCc` coin through `burnFrom(caller, fee)` (approve the Core first, a zero fee burns nothing); `inId` takes `outId`'s pile node (position, cost basis, acquisition time) and `outId` returns to the caller. `CreditSwapped(inId, outId, caller, fee)` |

a credit the engine sold through `sellCreditTo` is provisionally refused by every intake door for `returnLockSeconds` from the sale, regardless of later transfers: `sellForEth`, `sellForBalance`, `buyListing`, and `swapCredit` as the incoming credit return `ReturnLocked(id, until)`. the Core stores the buyer and sale time per id (buyer in the high 160 bits of the word, time in the low 96) and reads the live setting, so an owner change moves the end of every open lock. a secondary holder must also wait; this candidate changes decision 155's buyer-only rule and needs owner acceptance under decision 196. a lock of 0 permits immediate return. a credit the engine never sold passes. the reserve: `buyListing` refuses a quote above `ethPot` less `reserve()` and caps the caller payment at that room less the quote; `sellForEth` refuses a credit whose bid passes the room (`PotTooSmall`).

the first `migrate` retires the engine: these four doors and both composes revert `Retired`. `swapCredit` reverts `Retired` as well. forbidden targets, checked for adapters and their targets: Credits, Statements, the Core, the coin, the hook, the pool manager, the factory, the locker, the escrow, Permit2, the position manager, the universal router, the balance module and token.

the adapters: `SeaportBasicAdapter` prices a Seaport 1.6 basic order of one credit for native eth on an open route (the zone is ignored), with every additional recipient the offerer or an address on the adapter's allowlist (the OpenSea fee collector at launch; `setAllowedRecipient` by the Core's owner, additions refused after `lockAdapters`); `value` is the sum of every payout, so Seaport refunds nothing. `CreditStrategyAdapter` prices `nftForSale(id)` and calls `sellTargetNFT(id)`. a tip to the offerer or an allowlisted recipient can raise `value` up to the bid, which is what `sellForEth` pays.

## 7. compose

`compose()` composes the controller's page, the 80 oldest pile credits, with a format `keccak256(prevrandao, first id) % 8`; `compose(format)` with the given format, 0 to 7. `composeChosen(ids, format)` composes 80 pile credits the caller names: requires `chosenComposeOn`, a CC balance of at least `minCcToChoose`, and the newest named credit past its window. all three run one path: every credit is pulled from the pile (a missing or repeated id reverts), Statements mints to the Core, the record stores the cost (the sum of the credits' costs), the compose time, the rating (the sum of the credits' scores read from Statements) and the pause mark; `composerOf(sid)` stores the caller; `Composed(sid, ids, format, cost, price, caller)`, where `price` is the controller's `priceOf(sid)` read after the record is written with at most 400,000 gas, 0 when the read reverts, runs out of gas or answers short. the caller is paid `min(counted gas, 13,700,000) * min(basefee, callerMaxBaseFee) + callerFee` from the pot, 3 gwei and 0.0003 eth at launch (decision 138). while the credit sale is on, the page is ready only once its newest credit is past `window`.

`setHeldFormat(sid, format)`: anyone with at least `minCcToChoose` CC sets the format of a held statement through `Statements.setFormat`; works while paused and after `migrate`. the balance gate is read at the call. a buyer sets its own format after the sale.

## 8. the sale

the Core sells through two doors the controller alone calls. `sellTo(sid, buyer)` requires a held statement and `msg.value >= cost * saleFloorBps / 10,000`, sends the statement, takes `saleToArtistBps` and `saleToOperatorBps` off the payment and pushes them to the artist and the payee, books `saleToBuybackBps` of the rest to the buyback and the remainder to the pot. `sellCreditTo(id, buyer)` requires a pile credit and `msg.value >= cost * saleFloorBps / 10,000` (decision 154), stores the sale time for the return lock, sends the credit, takes the two cuts off the payment, books `creditCostToPotBps` of the cost (bounded by the rest) to the pot and the remainder of the rest to the buyback. both push the cuts with `forceSafeTransferETH` at 50,000 gas and both revert `Paused` while paused.

the controller prices. `pointRate = floorRate / RATE_SCORE` (wei per score unit; `RATE_SCORE` is 100 points in score units). `valueOf(points) = max(floorRate * points / RATE_SCORE, pool value of points * unit)`, where the pool term prices `points * unit` balance token base units at the balance pool's sqrt price and is 0 when the pool key or the module is unset, the unit read fails or exceeds `uint128.max`, the pool reads zero or a product exceeds 256 bits. the reference of a statement is `max(cost, valueOf(rating))`. the asking price is the reference times a bps curve: start `min(anchorBps * markupBps / 10,000, startCapBps)`, falling in a straight line to `saleFloorBps` of the reference over `decayHours` hours and resting there, computed at each call from the statement's age (`statementAge`, which excludes paused seconds) and the current settings. `atFloor(sid)` is gated by the controller's `ageEligibilityOn` (decision 155), false at launch. off, the launch rule compares the curve price with its floor and reads the reference. on, the rule is age only: true once `startBps() <= 10,000` or `statementAge(sid) >= decayHours * 3600`, reading no price or pool, so eligibility never restarts on a price move and never waits on a quote. at launch `saleFloorBps` is 10,000, so the two rules share the same age boundary. the anchor is 10,000 plus an excess that halves every `anchorHalfLife`; a `buy` of a statement costing at least `anchorMinCost` at a price above the anchor (in bps of the reference) moves it by `floor((saleBps - currentAnchor) * anchorMoveBps / 10,000)`, at most `startCapBps`. The launch fraction is 0 (decision 154): no sale moves the anchor and its clock stays untouched, so the start is `markupBps` of the reference, 150 percent, falling to the floor over the approved live `decayHours` 1 hour (constructor config 4 hours; decision 236); 10,000 would move it fully to the sale.

in value mode (section 4) `valueOf(points)` is `points * unit` balance token base units at `P_sell`, rounded up, with the floor rate term dropped, and reverts `NoQuote` while the quote is unusable; a statement's reference is `valueOf(rating) * floorMultBps() / 10,000`, the cost term dropped, and the curve rests at 10,000 of it instead of `saleFloorBps`; the final statement quote is still at least `cost * saleFloorBps / 10,000`, matching the Core's unconditional payment floor (decision 174); a credit's floor is `valueOf(score) * floorMultBps() / 10,000`, still at least `saleFloorBps` of the cost. `buy`, `buyCredit` and `buyCredits` call `refreshReference()` before they price.

`buy(sid)` takes `price`, forwards it to `sellTo`, refunds the excess last. the artist and operator cuts come off the payment inside `sellTo`.

the credit sale window (`saleOn`): every pile credit is for sale from its acquisition: `creditPrice(id)` falls in a straight line from `floor * startBps / 10,000` to `floor = max(cost, valueOf(score)) * floorMultBps() / 10,000` over `window` and rests there, where `floorMultBps() = max(10,000, (10,000 + creditSpreadBps) * balanceBidCapBps / 10,000)` reads the Core's cap live (11,000 at launch with the spread 1,000 of decision 155, 12,100 at the hard cap of 11,000; decisions 150, 152, 153, 155), and at least `saleFloorBps` of the cost, so the quote is always a price `sellCreditTo` takes. `valueOf(score)` is at least `floorRate * score / RATE_SCORE`, which is at least the per-credit eth bid `ethRate() * score / RATE_SCORE`, so a buy followed by `sellForEth` in the next second cannot profit, and the return lock refuses the credit for six hours at launch, time for the pool to move back after a manipulation. `buyCredit(id)` and `buyCredits(ids)` take the price each and forward it to `sellCreditTo`, which takes the two cuts off the top. `pastWindow(acquiredAt)` is true while the sale is off or `window` has passed; compose takes only credits past their window.

the pause: `setPaused(bool)` on the Core, owner only. It stops new Credit intake, listing purchases, composition, sales, Credit swaps and buybacks. Funding, fee booking, escrow collection and redemption remain callable. Both bid clocks stop earning growth during the pause, hold their remembered peaks and resume from the rate earned before it. `pausedTotal` accumulates closed pauses and `pausedSince` marks the open one; a statement's record stores the paused seconds at its compose, so its age `(now - composedAt) - (paused seconds since compose)` holds through a pause. The credit sale window and statement sale anchor run on wall time.

## 9. the balance side

in value mode (section 4) the eth bid prices off the balance price state instance: an eth fill drops it, its clamp is `balanceBidCapBps`, and a module set leaves it. the balance bid never reads or moves it (decision 164).

the module holds the balance token; the Core holds none (decision 117). the module's interface adds `available()`, the token it commits to the bid (it may hold more and keep the rest for other uses), and `pay(to, amount)`, the Core only, which moves `amount` to `to` and reverts above `available()`. a redemption's proceeds stay in the module. the bid off (`balanceBidOn` false) is the accumulate mode: redemptions keep filling the module while `redeemOn`, and nothing leaves the module through the Core until the owner turns the bid on. the bid opens at the posted price when the owner turns it on. a module that spends its holding elsewhere halts the bid at `PotTooSmall`; the balance side is optional by design.

the balance bid is a flat posted price (decision 164): `balanceRate()` is `10,000 - balanceDiscountBps` bps of par in every mode, bid on or off, and `balanceBidOf(id)` is `score * unit * balanceRate() / 10,000`, 0 when no module is set, the unit read fails, or the payout plus its artist royalty exceeds `available()` (the view never advertises a payout the fill refuses). in value mode each credit's posted payout is lowered to the controller's `balanceClampFor(score, posted)`, the eth bid for the score valued in tokens at the protected sale price per token, so a filled payout is worth at most the eth bid when the engine sells the tokens and a cross-currency round trip cannot extract a spot that leads the reference (decision 165); the royalty and the `available()` bound follow the clamped payout, and `balanceBidOf` and `balanceBidForScore` report it, so the view equals the fill. outside value mode the posted payout is par minus the discount, unchanged. `sellForBalance` pays each credit that payout, unchanged across fills and over time, and pays the artist `buyToArtistBps` of it on top in balance token through a second module `pay` (decision 163); the running total of payouts and royalties must not pass `available()` (`PotTooSmall`). the route's payoff is known in the unit it is paid in (100 points redeem for 100 balance token), so the price is par less a margin, and the discount is also the buffer that keeps the engine's bid from being the binding arbitrage between the credit market and the balance token price (composition arbitrage pays whenever credits trade under par, the engine's bid only once they trade more than the discount under). the credit enters the eth pile at cost basis `floorRate * score / RATE_SCORE`, at least 1 wei. a credit the engine sold through `sellCreditTo` within `returnLockSeconds` is refused (`ReturnLocked`, section 6). `BalanceFill` logs each credit's payout, its royalty, and the available left after both. the balance price state instance (`State.balance`) remains as value mode's eth instance only: `balanceBidMarginBps` and `balanceBidCapBps` are its floor and cap, the value switch resets it, and a module set never touches it.

`redeemStatement(sid)`: requires a module and `redeemOn`, a held statement, and `atFloor(sid)` from the controller (a bounded read of 400,000 gas; a failure reads as not eligible). the Core forgets the statement, hands it to the module, calls `redeem(sid)`, and requires the module's balance token holding to rise by at least `rating * unit * (10,000 - redeemSlippageBps) / 10,000`, with `unit` the module's `unitPerPoint()` read in the call. a failed, zero or oversized unit read, or a holding that rises by less than that, reverts `BadModule`. the proceeds stay in the module. `redeemBurnBps` of the redeem burn pot moves to the buyback. the caller is paid like a composer.

`setBalanceModule(module)`, owner only, any number of times until `lockBalanceModule`: the module must have code, report a balanceToken with code, which may change at an explicit owner module set (decision 142), pass the forbidden list, report a valid unit at the set, and answer `available()`. Replacement leaves custody in the old module. For the same token, anyone may request `migrateInactiveReserve()` after the upstream gates open; the old production module sends only its unowed reserve to the current module bound to the same Core and retains artist debt. A different-token replacement leaves its old reserve in the old module until an explicit conversion or re-selection before lock. A pool key prices value while its currency matches the selected token. Set the replacement's complete pool key before resuming sales that rely on its value floor. Leave the module door unlocked while replacements are wanted, and handle old-token reserves before locking the door. `lockBalanceModule` reverts while no module is set. `releaseRedeemBurn(amount)` moves reserve ETH to the buyback at any time.

the phase 2 treasury (decisions 245 to 251) is the module of the phase 2 release. its accounts, in balance base units with `B` the token it holds: `C` the claim reserve, `D` the recorded debt, `W = B - C - D` the working funds, `F` the minimum working balance; the rule is `B >= C + D` and `available()` is `max(W - F, 0)` while working payments are on, the treasury is the selected module of its unretired Core, it has not migrated and the token's ordinary transfers are open (finalized retirement, funding phase 3, the open block reached); else zero, never a revert. `redeem(sid)` needs the conversion switch, the selection and finalized retirement; it measures the receipt, requires the full rating times the unit, keeps every token and adds `conversionToClaimBps` of the receipt to `C`; the token releases its own reserve before ordinary transfers open, so conversion can run before working payments can. `pay` is the inherited debt accruing payment bounded by `available()`; `claim` pays recorded debt whatever the switches. `redeemCC(ccIn, recipient, minBalanceOut, deadline, expectedConfigEpoch)` snapshots `C` and the total supply `S`, reads the policy under a bounded static call, refuses an answer above `floor(ccIn * C / S)` or zero, burns exactly `ccIn` from the caller through the coin's `burnFrom`, requires the supply to fall by exactly that, pays the recipient and requires the receipt, all in one guarded transaction; there is no deferred user debt. one transient lock covers every mutation. `quoteRedemption` is the matching view with an `executable` flag.

## 10. buyback

`buyback()`, anyone, at most once per `buybackDelay` blocks: takes `min(buybackSlice, ethToBuyback)`, swaps exact eth in for coin on the canonical pool through the pool manager's unlock, burns the coin on the token, and pays the caller `min(counted gas, 600,000) * min(basefee, keeperMaxBaseFee) + keeperFee * spent / largest budget`. a slice under twice the largest payment reverts. the hook's skim on this swap reaches the Core during the swap and is fee income at the next book. there is no minimum output; the slice bound and the pool depth limit a sandwich (decision 51; lower the slice after a large price fall, RUNBOOK section 4).

The hard rule: ETH and Credits leave the Core at the owner's call only through `migrate`, to its successor. The balance token is held by an ownerless module. It leaves through the Core's `pay` and `redeem` paths; an inactive production module can send unowed same-token reserve to the current Core module through permissionless `migrateInactiveReserve()`, and a retired Core's module can send unowed reserve to its successor's same-token module through permissionless `migrateReserve()`. Recorded artist debt stays in the old module for `claim()`. Seller payout is the discounted posted bid, further clamped in value mode, with a royalty on top; the sum cannot exceed `available()`. Other outflows are bounded by the Credit bid, keeper fee and gas caps, caller fee and gas caps, sale floors, and the artist, operator and payee shares of payment or income. `setPayee` redirects the payee and operator amounts; `setArtist` redirects the artist amount.

what the rule does not cover, accepted by the owner (decisions 8, 9.4): the owner sets the price the engine pays, so a dishonest owner or a stolen key can sell credits to the engine at an inflated rate and drain the pot, set a module that returns dust for statements or a unit that overpays the balance bid, swap the controller and sell every statement at the floor, and move everything to a successor at once. holders trust the owner key fully. mitigations: a multisig owner, the events on every setter, the locks.
