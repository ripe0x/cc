## 1. the inventory gate

**counter.** `ethHeld` is written in exactly two places, `_compose` (plus one, only for the eth lane) and `_unhold` (minus one, only when the stored lane is eth). every caller of `_unhold` has already proven the statement held: `buyStatement` through `priceOf`, `exitStatement` through the held check, `overprint` through the held check of both and `base.lane == top.lane`. so each held eth statement was counted once on the way in and is removed once on the way out, the minus one cannot underflow, and the exit lane never touches it. `overprint` removes the top and keeps the base, which stays counted. a statement sent to the core from outside is accepted by `onERC721Received` but is not in `_statements`, so it is never counted and cannot be sold, exited or overprinted (`test_statementSentFromOutsideIsNotCounted`). the existing invariant (`ethHeld` equals the held eth statements, `Invariants.t.sol`) passed under `INVARIANT_ECON=gate5` and `recommended`, phase 1 and phase 2.

**checkpoints.** `_setEthHeld` checkpoints before the write only when the change crosses the gate, and `_gated()` is evaluated on the old counter, so the time before the crossing is credited under the old state and the time after under the new one. closing: the interval was open, the climb up to now is credited. reopening: the interval was gated, the checkpoint credits nothing and stamps now. `_syncFunded` is not needed after the crossing checkpoint, because the climb stops at the clamp, which is the same threshold as `funded`. both claims, "no climb is credited for any gated interval" and "no climb is lost for an ungated funded interval", are proven by `RateModelFuzz`: a model written independently of the core loop (tiers by days since the fill, clamp at the pot cap) is compared after every step of random walks of warps, fee intake, composes that close the bid and purchases that reopen it, and a fixed walk with five crossings. a mutant of the core without the crossing checkpoint fails both tests, so the test can fail.

**funded, clamp, lastFillTime.** unchanged by the commit except through `_gated()`. while gated the rate is frozen exactly like unfunded time, fees keep landing in the pot (`receive` and `skim` checkpoint with no climb), the clamp rises with the pot, and `lastFillTime` keeps running.

**reopening (E-2, E-1).** measured with `test_POC_reopenClimbsAtTheTopTier` after 5 days closed (rate in bps of the frozen rate, with the first tier in brackets): 1h 10799 (10099), 3h 12597 (10303), 6h 15868 (10615), 12h 25181 (11268). that is the unbounded no fill figure. what it can overpay is bounded by the climb speed times the time to the first fill, because the first fill resets the tier to 100 bps: 0.03 percent per 12 second block, 0.7 percent per 5 minutes, 7.7 percent per hour at 800 bps. a seller whose ask is under the stored rate fills in the first block, so the top tier costs close to nothing in exactly the case where overpaying matters. when asks are above the stored rate the engine is underbidding and the fast tier only clears it sooner.

is resetting `lastFillTime` at reopening the right fix? my view: no. it costs 54 bytes of the 123 byte margin (margin 69). it slows the recovery in a rising market from 800 to 100 bps an hour, which keeps the pot idle longer, against the purpose of the gate. it fixes a problem that the first fill already fixes. downsides: the next statement purchase resets the tier again, so a buyer can hold the climb at the first tier for the cost of a statement, and the unfilled time before the closing is forgotten. a pause semantics (shift the clock by the gated time) needs a new slot and more bytes.

the real exposure is E-1: the rate is not market linked and the gate makes the large idle pot the designed state (docs/SIMULATION.md section 9: 207 to 319 eth idle). at reopening the bid is the old rate, the hourly window opens with the grown pot (`windowPot = ethPot`), and a seller with credits cheaper than the stored rate can sell up to 20 percent of that pot in the first hour while the rate falls only `DROP_BPS` times the share, 4 percent at 2000. `test_POC_staleBidAfterTheGateAgainstAGrownPot`: pot 0.84 eth at reopening, 151 real credits sold, 20 percent of the pot spent, rate 9565 of 10000. projection (`test_POC_staleBidProjection`, market 40 percent under the stored rate, no new fees): after 8 hours 83 percent of the pot is gone and the rate is still 72 percent of the stored one, above the 60 percent market. the same exposure exists without the gate whenever the rate is stale, but the gate makes both ingredients (frozen rate, big pot) the normal state. the buyer of a statement chooses the moment of reopening, so a timing player can pick the moment the stored rate is furthest above the market. bounded by 20 percent of the pot per hour, not unbounded. a 20 percent haircut of the rate at reopening (72 bytes, margin 51) takes that much off the stale premium but also slows a legitimate recovery; i would not add it, i would sign the exposure off and keep the gate small.

**griefing and toggling.** anyone can compose when the controller says ready, so anyone can close the bid, and anyone can buy a statement at its auction price and reopen it. neither move pays by itself. closing freezes the climb, which no seller wants. reopening is paid for with a statement at 0.8x to 2x of its cost, which the buyer keeps and can resell. the profitable use is E-4: the buyer reopens, sells a full page of credits into the bid, composes the page and closes the bid in one transaction (`test_POC_reopenBackrunMonopoly`). the next honest seller finds `GateClosed`. what the bundle earns is the ceiling on the credits it sells, which is what any seller earns, plus the compose reimbursement. so the bid becomes a one slot race per statement sold, not a theft. combine it with E-1 and the racer also picks the moment.

**held closed forever.** in phase 1 (no exitModule, ControllerV1 never overprints) only `buyStatement` lowers the count. if no one buys at the floor (0.8x of cost) the bid stays closed for good: fees keep landing in the pot, `ethToBuyback` is fed by nothing (it is fed only by statement sales and exits), every sale reverts, `exitStatement` reverts `NoExitModule`, `overprint` reverts `NotReady` (`test_POC_phase1StallWithNoStatementBuyer`). that is E-3. with the default config it cannot happen, the bid never closes. with the recommended config it needs 20 unsold statements, 1600 credits. what un sticks it: a buyer, an exitModule (then every statement older than 72 hours can be exited by anyone, so in phase 2 the bid cannot be held closed for longer than the auction plus one keeper call, unless the module itself reverts), or a controller that overprints (at most 8 a day). the controller can be swapped through the 7 day timelock unless it was frozen, so freezing the controller must wait for the exitModule. with a controller that returns `ready` false compose reverts `NotReady`, the counter cannot rise and the gate cannot close, the pile just grows.

**other interactions, all clean.** `buyListing` checks the gate first and runs under `nonReentrant`, so the counter cannot change in the middle of the call (compose and `buyStatement` are `nonReentrant`, the hook push in `receive` books nothing while measuring). the hourly window is untouched by gating: the first spend after reopening opens a new window on the grown pot (this is the E-1 amount). the exit lane never counts (`test_exitLaneIsNeverGated` in Gate.t.sol and the lane check in `_unhold`). overprint is same lane only, so an eth pair lowers the count by one and an eth and exit pair reverts `BadOverprint`.

## 2. the auction multiples

price is `cost.mulDivUp(START * LEN - (START - FLOOR) * min(elapsed, LEN), LEN * 10000)`. checked by `PriceCurve` (four dial sets: default, recommended, both bounds at the low end, the closest start and floor the bounds allow; 257 fuzz runs each) against a reference written from the spec: exact at t = 0 (cost times start, rounded up) and at t = LEN (cost times floor, rounded up), never rising, never under `cost * floor / 10000` (the rounding is up, which is the safe direction for SPEC invariant 3), flat after LEN. overflow: the factor is at most 40000 * 259200, about 1e10, and `mulDivUp` is a full precision multiply, so only a cost near 2^200 can overflow, which is not reachable. overprint restarts the base clock and sums the costs, so the price restarts at the start multiple of the summed cost, unchanged by the commit. the dials are immutables, so no statement can be composed under other dials and there is nothing to migrate.

**floor under cost (E-6).** every place that could assume a sale returns at least the cost was re read. the 50/50 split is a split of the price whatever it is (`test_saleSplitHoldsAtTheFloor`). cost basis is only used to price the auction. the gas reimbursement is paid at compose, capped at 5 percent of the page cost and at 110 percent of gas, independent of the later sale: at 400 gwei the cap binds at 499 of 10000 (`test_POC_reimbursementCapAndTheFloorSale`), so a keeper's edge is at most the 10 percent over gas, and a lower floor adds no way to farm it. the reimbursement is part of the cost the statement sells against, so at the floor the pot gets back 0.4 of the cost (4000 bps in the test) and the buyback pot 0.4. the pot therefore loses 0.6 of the cost per statement cycle at the recommended floor, against 0.4 at the default, which is the price of selling more statements (docs/SIMULATION.md section 9).

**buying at the floor.** a buyer pays 0.8x of the engine's cost and holds a statement of rating R. in phase 2 the arbitrage is `R * unitPerPoint` of exitToken (what the module pays the core, and what anyone gets if the module lets a holder call it) against 0.8 of the cost in eth. the module's permission is outside this repo. the structural point: from 72 hours `exitStatement` is permissionless and the floor is reached at exactly 72 hours, so buyer and exiter race for the same statement, and whoever wins decides whether the engine takes eth at 0.8x or exitToken at the rating. with a floor over cost the buyer needed the exitToken to be worth more than 1.2x of cost, with 0.8 it needs 0.8x. the engine does not lose principal, it loses the option. the policy line in docs/SIMULATION.md section 9 (exit only when rating times unit is worth at least the floor) is the right guard and it is off chain.

## 3. DROP_BPS at 4000 and 1000

`r -= r * DROP * x / (10000 * p)` with `x <= p`, so one fill drops the rate by at most DROP bps (40 percent at 4000), the division rounds down, so the rate never drops more than the formula and never reaches zero (a fill of the whole pot leaves 60 percent). 40 fills in one block (`DropAt...` tests) equal the sequential formula exactly at both bounds, with a share of the pot spent of 12.9 and 12.7 percent. repeated fills inside a window are limited by the hourly cap (20 percent of `windowPot`), so the drop per hour is at most about 8 percent at 4000 and 4 percent at 2000 and 2 percent at 1000, and the product over many small fills approaches but does not exceed that. the rate has no floor, a long run of full cap hours at 4000 can take it far below the opening bid and the climb back is 100 bps an hour at first, which is the same shape as at 1000 but faster. overflow: `r` is at most about 5e16 and `x` at most the pot, so the product is far under 2^256.

## 4. constructor, views, loader, hash, default equivalence

constructor: start [15000, 40000], floor [6000, 12000], drop [1000, 4000], gate 0 or [5, 200], all revert with their own error (Econ.t.sol covers each side of each bound). the views are public immutables with the same names. the loader reads `.econ.<NAME>` with `parseJsonUint`, a config without the block reverts (`test_aConfigWithoutTheDialsDoesNotLoad`). `configHash` is the hash of the abi encoding of the whole `LaunchConfig`, so each of the four values changes it (`test_hashCoversEachDial`, plus `test_recommendedDiffersOnlyInTheEconKeys` in Config.t.sol, which shows the two files differ only in the four values). preflight rows and postflight reads match the constructor order `(owner, coin, controller, stack, rateStart, econ)` and `Resume` compares all four. the full `deploySystem` with postflight runs on the recommended file (`RecommendedDeployPath`).

default equals the previous commit: i extracted HEAD~1, ran one scenario (fills, 47 hours of climb, a compose, eight auction prices from 4x to 1.2x, a sale, 100 hours of climb, more fills) on both with zero basefee, and the output is identical line for line (rates, pots, buyback pot, cost, every price). the same numbers are pinned in `DefaultsReproduceThePreviousCommit`. with a nonzero basefee the compose cost and everything after it differ slightly because the counter writes add gas that the reimbursement repays (E-5).

## 5. runtime size

runtime 24453 bytes of 24576, margin 123 (reproduced). acceptable for the deploy: the code size limit is a hard line that the fork deploy already clears, the Core is immutable and has no upgrade path, so the margin only limits what can still be added before the launch. what it costs: any fix from this review that needs more than 123 bytes does not fit without a cut. measured costs: reset of `lastFillTime` 54, haircut 72, E-5 guard saves 10. if a later fix needs 300 bytes, cut in this order, each measured on this commit:

| cut | bytes freed | cost |
|---|---|---|
| make the three dial getters (`AUCTION_START_X`, `AUCTION_FLOOR_X`, `DROP_BPS`) internal | 210 | postflight and Resume lose the read back of those three, so they would verify through the etherscan constructor args and a behaviour probe instead |
| `ethHeld` and `INVENTORY_GATE` getters internal | 111 | operators lose the gate watch view, bad |
| one `econ()` tuple getter in place of four | 96 | script change only, smallest semantic cost |
| `optimizer_runs` 200 to 100 | 149 | no source change, but every byte of the reviewed code changes, the whole suite must rerun |

the first thing to cut is the `econ()` tuple (96 bytes, script only), then the three dial getters. do not touch `optimizer_runs` after the reviews. the constructor checks cost no runtime bytes (they live in the initcode), so the dead `floor < start` check can stay.

## 6. what was removed

`FEE_BPS` and `CREATOR_BPS` were informational, no logic read them. `RATE_START_MIN` and `RATE_START_MAX` lost their getters, the bounds are the shared constants in `Interfaces.sol`. grep over src, script, test, sim, docs and the json files: no code reads any of the four names off the Core (`ControllerV1` uses `pileSize` and `pilePage`, the scripts use the shared constants, the sim keeps its own copies, E-10 for the stale SPEC sentence). `pilePage`: `PilePageEquivalence` fuzzes lane, start (zero, a pile id, a junk id, the head), and n (zero, small, 80, huge) against a reference that walks `pileNext` like the old code, 301 runs, equal in every case where the start belongs to the lane asked about. the one difference is E-7. the new version allocates at most the pile size, so a huge n cannot blow memory, which is the point.
